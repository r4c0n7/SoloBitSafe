Your Bitcoin keys stay on a device you can hold.

SoloBitSafe turns a LilyGo T-Display-S3 into a keystore. The app on your phone or computer builds the transaction, the device shows what you are about to sign, and nothing leaves until you press BTN1. In between, it mines solo.

Current version V1.0.8b, released 16 October 2026.

Beta. Real mainnet payments sent and confirmed, every signature cross-checked by Electrum and embit, updates signed and protected against downgrades. Not yet independently audited, and Secure Boot is prepared but not yet enabled, so keep larger amounts on an established hardware wallet for now.

How a payment gets signed

  1. The app builds it

    On Android, Windows or Linux you pick a recipient and an amount. The app is watch-only and has no key.

  2. The device shows it

    The receipt appears on the display: full recipient address, amount and fee, checked by the device itself.

  3. You press BTN1

    Only a press on the device releases the signature. Contacts, updates and restores need it too.

  4. The app sends it

    The signed transaction goes back over USB, Bluetooth or Wi-Fi and the app broadcasts it.

What it does

Wallet

  • BIP39 seed with 12 or 24 words, encrypted on the device. It never leaves the device and is shown only on its display.
  • Main address m/84'/0'/0'/0/0. Receiving also on Taproot, nested SegWit and legacy; sending to every address type.
  • Connects over USB, Bluetooth LE with the passkey on the display, or HTTPS with a pinned certificate.
  • Works with Sparrow, Electrum and HWI, which detect it as a Jade-compatible hardware wallet.
  • Address book, auto-lock after five minutes, growing lockout after wrong passphrases.

Miner

  • About 380 KH/s from the ESP32-S3 hardware SHA unit plus a software core.
  • Every share and best difficulty is recomputed independently before it counts.
  • Solo only: your pool plus a solo fallback such as solo.ckpool.org.
  • Mining pauses while the wallet is unlocked, so signing always has the chip to itself.
  • An honest number: an ESP32 finds a block roughly once in 10¹³ days.

App

  • Android, Windows and Linux, in English, German, Spanish and French – the same language as the device display and the web dashboard.
  • Balance, history, coins, addresses, contacts and PSBT files in one place.
  • Fee bump (RBF), cancel and CPFP; change always returns to the main address.
  • Several wallets, app lock with fingerprint or Windows Hello.

Technical details

Hardware
LilyGo T-Display-S3: ESP32-S3, 16 MB flash, 8 MB PSRAM, 320×170 display, two buttons
Seed storage
Argon2id, an eFuse HMAC pepper burned into the chip, AES-256-GCM
Standards
BIP39, BIP32, BIP44, BIP49, BIP84, BIP86, BIP174 (PSBT), BIP340 (Schnorr), BIP380 descriptors
Signing
Deterministic ECDSA with low S, Schnorr for Taproot; PSBT up to 16 KiB
Connections
USB serial, Bluetooth LE Secure Connections, HTTPS with certificate pinning
Confirmation
BTN1 for every signature, contact change, update and restore
Updates
Over the air, signed with Ed25519, no downgrades, automatic rollback
Secure Boot
Secure Boot v2 and flash encryption prepared, not yet enabled on the reference device
Tests
501 firmware host tests; transactions cross-checked by Electrum
App
Flutter with BDK; Electrum or Esplora servers; Android, Windows (MSI and portable), Linux
Based on
NerdMiner V2 (MIT) and trezor-crypto (MIT)
